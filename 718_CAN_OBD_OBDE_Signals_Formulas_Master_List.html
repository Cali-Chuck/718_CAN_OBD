Master CAN & OBD Signal List for Base (2.0L) and S / GTS (2.5L)

The tables below enable 718 Boxster & Cayman owners who use mobile lap timers like RaceChrono, Harry's Lap Timer, Track Attack or others that support external Bluetooth OBD/CAN dongles like OBDLink MX+ to capture realtime telemetry for use on track and at home to produce videos and perform deep-dive analysis in platforms such as VBox Circuit Tools. Owners of hardwired in-car telemetry systems from VBox, Aim, RaceNavigator and others can also profit from this list.

Which Porsche Platforms Are Covered

This page is the work of many contributors who drive a variety of different 718 configurations. However, the signals published below are only validated on 2.0L & 2.5L flat-four turbocharged 718s with a Continental 5WP4659401 ECU (Porsche PN 982.907.309).

In the future, we hope to publish a flat-six specific list valid for GTS 4.0, GT and Spyder platforms with Bosch MDG1 ECU. Some CAN and OBD Extended (OBD-E) channels are common between GT and non-GT cars, but most are not. Where we have confirmed that a given OBD-E DID applies to Bosch it has been noted. We welcome contributions from flat-six owners to expand the tables.

Our research suggests that a large number of 718 CAN signals also work on 981 models, as well as 991 and 992 platforms and possibly some Panamera and Porsche SUV model years. We have reason to believe – based on Volkswagen technical documentation – that since 2016 or so VAG has converged on a set of "common" CAN PIDs across multiple brands. It is likely in particular that some CAN PID blocks also apply to Audi B8, C7, D4 and Q5 platforms as well as to some VW branded models.

Understanding Our Table Format & Types of Telemetry

Because no single source contains all the necessary goodies, we have attempted to present a single consolidated view showing how to reach each vehicle signal of interest, and by which routes. We have done exhaustive research on CAN broadcast, standard OBD-II Mode $01, and manufacturer UDS Mode $22. Here's a quick overview:

  • "OBD Basic" Mode $01 is the "standard" OBD mode most people are familiar with. On the Continental ECU there are 32 supported Protocol IDs ("PIDs"). Highly desirable signals like current gear and brake pressure are NOT available via Mode $01.
  • "OBD Extended (OBD-E)" Mode $22 is a manufacturer-specific, ECU-specific set that piggybacks a different protocol called UDS over the very same OBD port. This is what PIWIS / Autel / ThinkDiag / etc. use to query your engine, change codes, etc. It's what tuners like Cobb use for datalogging and flashing new maps. Current Gear and brake pressure ARE available via OBD-E on flat-four ECUs. Almost any piece of data you could think of needing is available via OBD-E, the problem is speed. OBD is transaction based — with every single inquiry for every signal having to be requested, then wait for a response, over and over in perpetuity. Beyond 10-15 signals OBD and OBD-E can become unusable.
  • "CAN Bus" is the native high-speed network in modern vehicles that interconnects all the different computers. In a 718 there are no fewer than five different CAN buses, with the Drive CAN being the key. Whereas both OBD Basic and OBD-E signals can be sent over your standard OBD port under the dash, accessing the CAN bus requires physically tapping into cabling somewhere in the car. Some contributors have tapped the Chassis CAN instead of the Drive CAN — the tables below provide a mapping of which signals are on which bus. CAN can be over 50X faster than OBD, and it is a highly efficient "broadcast" system that sends a huge volume of data every second. For more advanced users this is the place to be, but requires some modest physical modification of some wires in your vehicle with the attendant implied risks. But hundreds — maybe thousands — of Porsche owners have successful CAN taps.

One of us (@Cali_Chuck) uses both CAN and OBD-E simultaneously with two OBDLink MX+ sending telemetry to RaceChrono. Because while CAN is far richer than Basic OBD, on 718s it does not have a number of very desirable fields for anyone doing engine tuning. For example, turbo boost (actual) is available over CAN but the commanded boost (set point) is only on OBD-E. It is very possible to run multiple OBDLink MX+ devices concurrently with a lap timer on an iOS or Android device.

If you're here chances are you already found the very active CAN / OBD contributor community on Porsche 718 Forum, or perhaps at the RaceChrono forum. But if not, check them out for deep dive discussions from 718 owners on their builds, often with detailed installation instructions and photographs to get you started.
